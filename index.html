Method and limits

How scores are built

  • Each factor is a 1–10 rating drawn from public indicators available through mid-2026. Examples include CPCB air quality, NCRB crime rates, the TomTom traffic index, Swachh Survekshan ranks, Ookla speeds, IMD climate normals, NIRF institutions, and rent and price trackers.
  • A persona score is the weighted average of the 17 factor ratings, scaled to 100.
  • Weights show what each persona cares about. For example, retirees put 18% on healthcare and 0% on jobs, while remote workers put 15% on internet and 0% on commute.

Read with care

  • Ratings are judgments, not live data. They compress many sources into one number, and they will drift as conditions change, such as new metro lines or water shortages.
  • Crime data has reporting bias. Cities where more people report crimes can look less safe than they are.
  • City boundaries vary. Delhi, Gurugram and Noida are scored separately even though many people live in one and work in another.
  • Smaller cities score well when jobs carry little weight. For remote workers and retirees, smaller cities rank high because jobs barely count. That reverses quickly for anyone who needs a local job.
